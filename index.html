Teachers
Policy 1
What we collect

Only what the tool needs: your name and roll number, plus your quiz scores, subjects, units and weak areas. Your chats and the questions you ask the AI are never recorded or shared.

Policy 2
Why we collect it

To show you your progress, adapt your quizzes and let your teacher know when you may need help. It is never used for ranking, advertising or selling to anyone.

Policy 3
Who can see it

You, and the teacher for your branch. The college keeps control of its own data, and the company that supplies the tool may not use it for its own purposes. After every quiz you can see exactly what was sent.

Policy 4
Your choices

Personalized learning is your choice, and the trial runs only with your consent. Switch it off for private mode, where nothing is recorded, shared or personalized. You can delete your data at any time from your Home screen, and it is removed from this device and from your teacher’s view.

Policy 5
Fairness and accuracy

The assistant is checked for bias through regular audits, and it can still be wrong. A check does not prove it is fair, so please do not trust it blindly. A low score or an alert is a signal, not a judgment about you. If something looks unfair or incorrect, tell your teacher or use the Feedback tab.

Policy 6
Learning first

By default it gives hints and guiding questions. Full answers are for after you have tried the problem yourself. Harder topics and tougher questions are optional, for students who are interested in the course.

Policy 7
Teachers decide

The AI only supports teachers. An alert is a prompt to talk to you, never a verdict. Teachers are asked to check in with students themselves and not rely on alerts alone, because an alert can miss a student who needs help. A teacher makes every decision about you.

Policy 8
Security and retention

Data is kept only while you use the tool and is deleted when you ask. Access is limited to your teacher. No system is ever completely secure, so a real rollout would add encryption, regular security checks, fixes for any weaknesses found, and a clear contract with the supplier.

Policy 9
Feedback and review

A student feedback committee reads the anonymous feedback sent from the Feedback tab. Policies, bias checks and security are reviewed from time to time, and changes are introduced step by step.

Policy 10
Trial first

The tool starts as a trial in one or two departments, with student consent. It spreads to all departments gradually, and only after feedback has been acted on.

Plan A: trial, then grow

  1. Trial in one or two departments, with student consent.
  2. Strict privacy rules and user rights from the start, with regular bias audits.
  3. Teachers supervise every AI decision and alert.
  4. Collect student and faculty feedback and fix problems.
  5. Extend to all departments gradually.

Plan B: if Plan A fails

  1. Use a smaller, optional version that does not track students or send “at risk” alerts. Private mode in this prototype works this way.
  2. If the company cannot give enough privacy and security, look for another company.
  3. Or use a tool built and run by the college, so the data stays inside the college.

Workshop: using AI as a tool

A short guide for students and faculty. No sign-in needed.

For students

Try first

Attempt the problem before you ask. The assistant gives hints first and a full answer only after you have tried.

Ask for a hint, not the answer

Keep Hint mode on and ask what to try next. That is how you build the skill the exam will test.

Check what it says

The assistant can be wrong. Compare with your notes and textbook, and ask your teacher when you are unsure.

Know your data

Your name, roll number and quiz results go to your teacher. Chats are never shared. Private mode records nothing.

Say when something feels off

If a hint, a quiz or an alert looks unfair, send anonymous feedback from the Feedback tab.

For faculty

An alert is a prompt

Talk to the student before deciding anything. The AI supports you and does not decide for you.

Look beyond the alerts

A student who needs help may not be flagged, and a student who is doing well may be. Keep checking in yourself.

Watch for bias

If recommendations look uneven between students, report it so the next audit can look at it.
